Three marks grown from the land

Each concept starts from farming: a leaf, a field, a terraced hillside. Each also works for maps and data, so the brand can grow into climate and geospatial work later. All three share one custom wordmark, where both As are drawn as leaves and the first one holds a seed.

Chosen · C + A wordmark

Terraces with leaf A

The Terraces symbol from concept C, paired with the custom wordmark whose As are drawn as leaves. The symbol carries the land and the map; the leaf As carry growth inside the name itself. This is now the logo on the website.

A · My pick

Leaf A

The A of SHAZA becomes a pointed leaf, and the crossbar becomes a seed. One shape says three things at once: a letter of the name, a young leaf, and an arrow pointing up.

Why it works

  • The symbol and the wordmark are the same shape, so they feel like one system.
  • It is the simplest of the three. It stays clear at 16px and is easy to engrave, stitch or stamp.
  • A leaf on its own is a common farming icon. A leaf that is also a letter with a seed inside is not.

Watch out for

  • It says "growth" more than "data". The data side has to come from the typography and the website.

Field

A farm plot seen from above, the way a satellite sees it. The crop rows curve with the shape of the land, and one row, in gold, is planted first. The circle also reads as a globe.

Why it works

  • The strongest link to what SHAZA actually does: looking at land through data and maps.
  • It stretches naturally to climate and earth observation later.

Watch out for

  • It has no link to the letters of the name, so people learn it more slowly.
  • The rows need care to stay crisp at very small sizes. The small version uses fewer, thicker rows.
C

Terraces

The hills of Rwanda and Burundi are farmed in terraces that follow the curve of the slope. Seen from above, they look like contour lines on a map. Here four terraces bend into the letter S, with a seed at the top of the hill.

Why it works

  • The most local of the three: terraced hills are part of the real landscape SHAZA starts in.
  • Contour lines are a map convention, so it also says "geospatial data".

Watch out for

  • Four lines blur below 24px. The small version uses two.
  • Without the explanation, some people may see stripes rather than terraces.
Next step

Pick one, or mix them

I recommend A, Leaf A: it is the simplest, it stays clear at every size, and the symbol is a letter of the name. If you want the data and map side to show more, B, Field is the strongest alternative. Every concept uses the same leaf wordmark, so you can mix any symbol with it.
